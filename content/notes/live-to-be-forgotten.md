People often optimize their lives with the end goal of being remembered. But why on Earth would that ever be a goal worth achieving? Anybody can be remembered. Remember Osama Bin Laden? I bet you do! Of course it's an extreme example, but the point is clear: being remembered is not inherently good.

So, let's make the distinction, people want to be remembered in a positive light when they die. Okay cool. But the question remains... why? 

In a modern context, there is no logical incentive to care. Unless you're somehow living in 2026 as an ancient Egyptian and worried about being remembered to sustain your [Ka](https://www.globalegyptianmuseum.org/glossary.aspx?id=204) for the afterlife, not even religion offers a compelling reason to strive for legacy. In fact, most religions want you to do something of the opposite: giving up everything and dedicating your time on earth to God alone, *yada yada*.

Since there's no logical explanation, we must turn to the illogical motivators. Ego. It must be that pride fuels the desire to be remembered once one is gone.

> Do not get me wrong, I am incredibly prideful. But I cannot relate to this at all.

My pride drives me to be the best I can possibly be **while I'm still here**. Perhaps it's short-sighted of me, but I simply do not care what happens on this planet once I am gone. But to be truthful, I feel those who want a legacy are really the selfish ones. Generally, the goal of building a legacy doesn't stem from a desire to actually help the future, it's an attempt to get people who haven't even been born yet to spend their time thinking about *you*. It's just vanity masquerading as altruism. 

Now morally that still does not mean I have the right or the desire to contribute to creating a worse world, as I still hold value in my actions today. However, if the world **does** end up worse after I'm gone? *Womp womp*. 

I reject the idea that we need a post-mortem reputation to be ethical. My incentive to uphold ethical standards is the reality of the life I am living now. If I treat people poorly, I face those consequences today. My motivation for being "good" is not for some legacy, it's for the present.

How did we get here? Oh, right, being remembered. All I'm saying is I have yet to hear a sensible reason for why somebody would prioritize legacy. Innovation and discovery are rarely driven by altruism for future generations; they are driven by solving the problems of the *now*. I am here to solve the problems of my time, just like everyone before me. Do you think Newton discovered calculus because he wanted to make sure Max Planck could invent quantum mechanics a few centuries later? No! He just wanted to know how planets and stuff moved. Point is, legacy is a motivator for the selfish, and perhaps somewhat unintuitively, those who live in the present are the unselfish ones.

Maybe I am just overlooking something, but I am comfortable with that for now.
